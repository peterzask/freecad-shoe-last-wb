● That asymmetry is the heart of it. The medial side of a last isn't a mirror of the lateral — the medial arch creates that characteristic
  inward sweep at the waist that then flows forward with convexity toward the big toe joint, while the lateral side runs straighter and
  slightly concave from heel to little toe. C1 and C2 encoding that difference independently is why they can't just be ±y offsets of each
  other.

  The E→joint-line geometry is a nice anchor — E gives you the high point of the shoulder in the instep region, and the 10mm-forward-of-joint
  reference gives you the inflection point where the toe box begins to diverge. That span is where the C curves will do the most work
  shaping the last's character.

  When you're ready to draw those profile curves, the key question will be where C1/C2 sit in height relative to the highwater marks at a few
  key stations — heel, instep, joint, and toe end. Even rough control points there will let you see whether the surface follows Koleff's
  intent. We can refine from what the model shows.

● The issue is on line 239. extra_point_K is defined as:

  extra_point_K = (self.J2 + self.H2 + App.Vector(0,20,0)) * 0.5

  That point is further from the centerline (more negative y) than J2. So _l_uJ2exK = extra_point_K - J2 points away from the centerline, and
  adding half of it moves further out still — opposite of what you want.

  To pull toward the centerline, negate the direction:

  def temp_a():
      _l_uJ2exK = extra_point_K - self.J2
      return self.J2 - _l_uJ2exK.multiply(0.5)   # subtract → toward center

  Or more directly — just scale J2's y toward zero:

  _temp = App.Vector(self.J2.x, self.J2.y * 0.5, 0)

  That second form is simpler and makes the intent obvious: same x as J2, half the lateral offset. You can change the 0.5 to tune how far
  toward the centerline it pulls.
